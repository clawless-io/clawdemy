## Going deeper

A short list of durable resources for readers who want more than the lesson covers.

- **[Clawless Computer: Architecture](https://clawless.ai/architecture/).** Grounds the architectural-alternative paragraph. Maps where the data goes and what is kept on the user's own machine, for a desktop AI app whose computer talks directly to the AI provider with no intermediate vendor server. Useful for readers who want the contrast case worked all the way out (where data lives, what the desktop app is responsible for, what the AI provider is responsible for). Read it alongside this lesson's seven-step path to see the two architectures side by side.

- **[Anthropic Privacy Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy).** The full privacy policy of one major AI provider, cited in this lesson for the canonical Inputs and Outputs vocabulary definition. Worth reading as a worked example of what a vendor privacy policy actually looks like, and as practice at reading one against the seven-step path. The "Inputs and Outputs" terminology appears throughout the policy and is the load-bearing vocabulary for the rest of the document.

- **[Mozilla Privacy Not Included: AI chatbot category](https://web.archive.org/web/20250408105319/https://foundation.mozilla.org/en/privacynotincluded/articles/how-to-protect-your-privacy-from-chatgpt-and-other-ai-chatbots/).** Mozilla's curated overview of AI chatbot privacy postures across the major vendors. Cited in lesson 1.1 for the multi-layer settings problem; cited here for the multi-layer settings problem it documents. Mozilla moved Privacy Not Included on 11 August 2026 and continues the work as Product Reviews in its Nothing Personal magazine, so the original URL now serves a notice rather than the article. Mozilla directs readers to the Internet Archive for the old material, which is what this citation points at: the capture of 8 April 2025. Because it is a 2025 snapshot it can no longer serve as a starting point for comparing what vendors say today; the Transparency Hub below does that job.

- **[Harvard Berkman Klein Transparency Hub](https://hub.transparency.berkmancenter.org/).** Searchable, current comparison of how named consumer products describe their data practices, and how those descriptions have changed over time. This is the live replacement for the role the archived Mozilla article used to play here: Mozilla itself points readers to this project for tracking policy change. Use it when you want today's wording from a specific vendor rather than a snapshot.

## Source limitations

These sources are good. Naming what each one is weaker on helps you use them well.

**Clawless Computer KB: "What is Under the Hood".** This is a sister-project knowledge-base chapter written by the Clawless team about Clawless Computer. It is honest about Clawless Computer's specific architecture (the desktop app talks directly to the AI provider; no Clawless Computer server holds logs of requests). It is *not* a neutral comparative survey of all AI tool architectures. The chapter is the canonical worked example for one specific architectural shape; it is not the only such shape, and the typical consumer-AI shape this lesson walks first is the more common one. Read the chapter for the contrast case, not as a buyer's guide.

**Anthropic Privacy Policy.** The Anthropic privacy policy is cited here for terminology grounding (Inputs/Outputs vocabulary). It is not presented as a positive or negative exemplar. This is one specific vendor's privacy policy, captured at a point in time. Vendor privacy policies revise on their own cadence (often quarterly, sometimes more frequently). The Inputs and Outputs vocabulary the lesson cites is industry-standard and appears in many vendors' policies, not only Anthropic's; the verbatim quote attributed here is one of several available sources of the same vocabulary. If Anthropic restructures their policy substantively, the verbatim quote may no longer appear in the form cited; the 6-month review cadence is intended to catch that. The lesson uses Anthropic's wording because it is clear and stable, not because Anthropic's policy is exemplary or non-exemplary in any other respect.

**Mozilla Privacy Not Included AI chatbot article.** Mozilla is a privacy-advocacy organization with a consistent pro-privacy point of view. Their coverage highlighted risks more than tradeoffs and was updated on Mozilla's own publication cadence rather than continuously. The article is now fixed in time, so treat every per-product specific in it as historical. The article is a useful snapshot for understanding the vendor-policy landscape but is not exhaustive (it does not cover every AI vendor) and may be partially out of date on specific settings paths for any given product. Use as one input alongside the vendors' own documentation, not as the only source.
