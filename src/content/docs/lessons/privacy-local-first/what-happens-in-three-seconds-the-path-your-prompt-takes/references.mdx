## Going deeper

A short list of durable resources for readers who want more than the lesson covers.

- **[Clawless Computer: Architecture](https://clawless.ai/architecture/).** Grounds the architectural-alternative paragraph. Maps where the data is designed to go and what is kept on the user's own machine, for a desktop AI app whose computer talks directly to the AI provider with no intermediate vendor server. Useful for readers who want the contrast case worked all the way out (where data lives, what the desktop app is responsible for, what the AI provider is responsible for). Read it alongside this lesson's seven-step path to see the two architectures side by side.

- **[Anthropic Privacy Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy) (effective 10 September 2026).** The full privacy policy of one major AI provider, cited in this lesson for the canonical Inputs and Outputs vocabulary definition. The quoted sentences come from the policy's definition of Inputs and Outputs; the training statement the lesson paraphrases is in its section on how the company uses personal data, which says Inputs and Outputs may be used to train and improve its models unless you opt out in your account settings, with exceptions for conversations flagged for safety review and material you report. Worth reading as a worked example of what a vendor privacy policy actually looks like, and as practice at reading one against the seven-step path. The "Inputs and Outputs" terminology appears throughout the policy and is the load-bearing vocabulary for the rest of the document.

- **[Mozilla Privacy Not Included: "How to Protect Your Privacy From ChatGPT and Other AI Chatbots"](https://web.archive.org/web/20250408105319/https://foundation.mozilla.org/en/privacynotincluded/articles/how-to-protect-your-privacy-from-chatgpt-and-other-ai-chatbots/).** Mozilla's practical guide to the privacy settings of popular AI chatbots. Cited in lesson 1.1 for the multi-layer settings problem; listed here as background on the settings you will meet when you read your own tool's policy. Mozilla moved Privacy Not Included on 11 August 2026 and continues the work as Product Reviews in its Nothing Personal magazine, so the original URL now serves a notice rather than the article. Mozilla directs readers to the Internet Archive for the old material, which is what this citation points at: the capture of 8 April 2025. Because it is a 2025 snapshot it can no longer serve as a starting point for comparing what vendors say today; the Transparency Hub below does that job.

- **[Harvard Berkman Klein Transparency Hub](https://hub.transparency.berkmancenter.org/).** Searchable, current comparison of how named consumer products describe their data practices, and how those descriptions have changed over time. This is the live replacement for the role the archived Mozilla article used to play here: Mozilla's Privacy Not Included notice points readers to this project, which it calls its partners at Harvard, for tracking how policies change. Use it when you want today's wording from a specific vendor rather than a snapshot.

## Source limitations

These sources are good. Naming what each one is weaker on helps you use them well.

**Clawless Computer: "Architecture".** This is a sister-project page written by the Clawless Computer team about Clawless Computer. It describes Clawless Computer's specific design (the desktop app talks directly to the AI provider; no Clawless Computer server is in the path holding chats), and the product is not publicly released yet, so the page describes the design rather than a shipped release. It is *not* a neutral comparative survey of all AI tool architectures. The page is the worked example for one specific architectural shape; it is not the only such shape, and the typical consumer-AI shape this lesson walks first is the more common one. Read the page for the contrast case, not as a buyer's guide.

**Anthropic Privacy Policy.** The Anthropic privacy policy is cited here for terminology grounding (Inputs/Outputs vocabulary). It is not presented as a positive or negative exemplar. This is one specific vendor's privacy policy, captured at a point in time. Vendor privacy policies revise on their own cadence. This one already has: the version effective 10 September 2026 rewrote the Inputs and Outputs definition, and the lesson's quote was updated to match it on 11 November 2026. If the policy changes again, the verbatim quote may no longer appear in the form cited; the 6-month review cadence is intended to catch that. Similar words appear in some other vendors' documents, alongside the different vocabulary the lesson names. The lesson uses Anthropic's wording because it is clear, not because Anthropic's policy is exemplary or non-exemplary in any other respect.

**Mozilla Privacy Not Included AI chatbot article.** Mozilla is a privacy-advocacy organization with a consistent pro-privacy point of view. Their coverage highlighted risks more than tradeoffs and was updated on Mozilla's own publication cadence rather than continuously. The article is now fixed in time, so treat every per-product specific in it as historical. The article is a useful snapshot for understanding the vendor-policy landscape but is not exhaustive (it does not cover every AI vendor) and may be partially out of date on specific settings paths for any given product. Use as one input alongside the vendors' own documentation, not as the only source.
