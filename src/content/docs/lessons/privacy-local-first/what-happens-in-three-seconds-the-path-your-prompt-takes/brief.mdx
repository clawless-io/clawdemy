## What this lesson is

Lesson 2.1 is the opening lesson of Track 6 Phase 2 (data-flow mental model). It is a concept lesson: its job is to give Sarah a mental picture of what actually happens when she clicks Send in an AI tool. The phase's three lessons each take a different cut at the same path: 2.1 walks the path itself, 2.2 walks who sees what at each step, 2.3 walks what persists and what does not. This lesson is the scaffold the other two annotate.

The lesson body runs approximately 14 minutes. All five derivative artifacts (this brief, summary, practice, cheatsheet, references) are complete and consistent with the body.

## Who this lesson is for

The primary persona is Sarah in the Aisha configuration, continuing from lessons 1.1 and 1.2. Aisha has named her three worries (1.1), written her seed paragraph (1.2), and put a sticky note on her laptop reading *check what's in the tab before you paste*. This lesson finds her on Friday morning at her desk, about to use the school AI tool for the first time, wanting to understand what happens between her keyboard and the model before she pastes anything real.

The Khan-warmth register from 1.1 and 1.2 carries forward. The technical content of Phase 2 (CDN, model service, plaintext, streaming) is harder to deliver in Khan-warmth than the orientation content of Phase 1, so the lesson uses Aisha's concrete situation as the spine and introduces each technical term in the moment she would encounter it, never as a definition for its own sake.

## What capability it delivers

**The one end-state capability** (per `Doc/curriculum/track-6/source-to-phase-mapping.md` Phase D lock): the student can describe in plain language the end-to-end path of a single prompt-and-response, from her keyboard to the model and back.

The supporting moves the lesson body teaches (each named in `learning_outcomes`) build to that end-state:

- Name the seven steps in plain language (the keyboard, the browser, the network, the CDN, the model service, the reply path, the chat box)
- Identify the CDN as the first piece of vendor infrastructure that touches a request, and explain what metadata it sees (request size, timestamp, geographic region) versus what it does not see (message contents)
- Define plaintext as the state of a message inside the model service and explain why encryption in transit does not change it
- Recognize Inputs and Outputs as the canonical vendor vocabulary every major privacy policy uses
- Distinguish path (structural reality) from policy (vendor commitment) as two separate layers that must be read against each other

These are capabilities, not facts. The goal is not that Sarah knows what a CDN is in the abstract; it is that the next time she reads a vendor's privacy policy or settings page she has a mental picture of what the words on the page are talking about.

## How it advances Phase 2

Phase 2 (data-flow mental model) has one job: give Sarah a concrete mental picture of what an AI tool sees when she uses it. The phase capability is *the student can trace where her data goes when she pastes a prompt and identify where it is exposed to what parties.* This lesson delivers the first half (the trace itself); lessons 2.2 and 2.3 deliver the second half (who sees what, when it persists).

Without 2.1, lessons 2.2 and 2.3 have no scaffold. The seven-step path is the bone structure the rest of the phase attaches to. The two new terms introduced in this lesson that the phase reuses are *CDN* (returned to in 2.2 when naming the parties) and *Inputs and Outputs* (returned to in 2.3 when discussing persistence).

The lesson explicitly defers three downstream concerns:

- Threat categorization of what could go wrong at each step (Phase 3)
- The rubric for reading a vendor's actual policy commitments against the path (Phase 4)
- Architectural alternatives that change the path itself (Phase 5)

Each downstream concern is named with a one-line forward-reference so Sarah knows the worry has a path, not just a name.

## New terms introduced (closed-vocabulary list for Phase 2 scope)

The lesson body introduces these terms and defines each one in-body before any practice or derivative reuses it. Phase 2's closed-vocabulary discipline depends on this list being accurate.

- **Round trip:** the full journey of a single message-and-reply pair through the path.
- **CDN (Content Delivery Network):** the vendor's geographically distributed front-door infrastructure. Architecturally the first place inside the vendor's perimeter where messages exist in plaintext; what gets logged versus what passes through is a policy choice.
- **Request metadata:** the wrapper around the message contents: timestamp, rough geographic region, message size, sometimes browser type. Not the contents themselves.
- **Model service:** the vendor software that runs the model: takes the message, hands it to the model, watches the reply being generated, sends the reply back out.
- **Plaintext:** the opposite of encrypted; readable text. Messages are plaintext inside the model service even when they were encrypted in transit.
- **Streaming:** the modern user-experience pattern of sending the reply back one piece at a time as the model generates it, rather than waiting for the full reply.
- **Inputs / Outputs:** one example of the vocabulary a vendor's privacy policy uses for the user's messages and the model's replies. Other vendors use different words for the same two things; the lesson uses Inputs and Outputs as a worked example, not as industry-standard terminology.
- **API (Application Programming Interface):** named in the architectural-alternative paragraph; the direct connection a vendor offers to developers and apps, where one piece of software talks to another without going through the vendor's chatbot website.
- **Context window:** named in the lesson for orientation; formalized in Lesson 2.3.

## Review cadence

Review every 6 months. The seven-step path is durable (the shape of consumer AI infrastructure does not change on a quarterly cadence). The Inputs and Outputs vendor vocabulary is durable as long as the cited vendor's privacy policy retains it; this is the part most likely to drift, so the 6-month review checks the verbatim quote against the live URL. If the cited vendor changes their vocabulary substantively, the quote needs re-anchoring (or a substitute vendor citation if the original drops the terms entirely).

**Coupled artifacts.** The verbatim Inputs and Outputs quote appears in both `lesson.mdx` (the Inputs and Outputs section) and `summary.mdx` (the Inputs and Outputs section). When the cited vendor revises their privacy policy in a way that changes the quote, both artifacts must be updated together. The cheatsheet, references, and practice flashcards reproduce the Inputs and Outputs vocabulary at the concept level but do not reproduce the verbatim quote and are not coupled to it.
