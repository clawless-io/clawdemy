## "See" means *technically able to observe*

The vendor's CDN technically sees your traffic. Whether it logs the contents is a separate question (the vendor's policy decides that).

- **The path:** what is structurally possible at each step.
- **The policy:** what the party at that step commits to doing with the ability.

Both layers exist. Both have to be read.

---

## Parties at each step

| Step | Parties | What they can see |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Keys to chat box** | You, your OS, your browser, any browser extensions | Whatever you type, in plaintext |
| **2. Browser onto the network** | Your local network operator (your school IT, your home router, the coffee shop wifi), your ISP, the public internet's routers | Connection metadata; encryption hides contents |
| **3. The CDN (vendor's front door)** | The CDN itself, the CDN engineering team, the vendor's site reliability engineers (SREs), a third-party CDN service if the vendor uses one | Message contents in plaintext (the CDN ends the encryption); what gets logged is a policy choice |
| **4. Inside the model service** | The model service software, the engineering team that maintains it, the platform's safety review process, vendor staff with debugging access | Message contents in plaintext, plus the reply as it is generated |
| **5. The model and its hardware** | The hardware operator (vendor or a cloud infrastructure provider the vendor pays) | Architecturally able to see contents; routine access constrained by contract |
| **6. The reply comes back** | Same parties as the forward direction, in reverse | Reply contents (same handling as the message) |
| **7. The reply appears** | You again, plus browser extensions, screen-sharing software, anyone sitting at your shoulder, anyone who later opens your conversation history | Whatever the reply contains |

## Controller and processor

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Controller** | The party that decides what data is collected and what it is used for. |
| **Processor** | The party that handles data on behalf of the controller. |

| Scenario | Controller | Processor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You use the vendor's website on your own initiative | The vendor | (not applicable) |
| Your employer has set up an enterprise account on the vendor's platform; you use it for work | Your employer | The AI vendor |

In the enterprise case, the employer's privacy policy is the one that applies first.

---

## Two access tiers and one variant

- **Consumer access.** The vendor's website. Seven-step path with a CDN front door. Most readers use AI tools this way.
- **API access.** The direct connection a vendor offers to developers and apps. The consumer-facing CDN is usually out of the path. Different parties along the way.
- **BYOK (Bring Your Own Key).** A variant of the API tier where an app routes your usage through *your* API account, not the app maker's. The AI provider sees your usage; the app maker may see less.

This is why some vendor policies list "consumer data" and "API data" as separate categories.

## Three pitfalls to dodge

| Pitfall | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| **Treating *no one sees this* as a default** | Some party sees your message at almost every step of the path. The right question is *which party* and *what is their policy*, not *whether* the path is observable. |
| **Confusing *the vendor doesn't train on this* with *the vendor doesn't store this*** | Two different commitments. A vendor can honestly say it does not train on your messages while still storing them in operational logs for a fixed retention window. |
| **Forgetting about the local side** | Browser extensions, screen-recording software, anyone at your shoulder, anyone who later opens your conversation history. The seven-step path describes the vendor side. Your local environment is its own list of parties. |

## Vocabulary to carry forward

- **"See" (in this lesson):** technically able to observe; not the same as routinely reading every message.
- **Site reliability engineers (SREs):** the people at the vendor who keep production systems running. May need debug access during incidents.
- **Safety review process:** automated classifier + human review of flagged content. The most common path by which a person at the vendor reads a specific conversation.
- **Consumer access vs API access:** two different paths to a vendor with different party lists.
- **BYOK (Bring Your Own Key):** API-tier variant; routes usage through your own API account.
- **Controller:** decides what data is collected and what it is used for.
- **Processor:** handles data on behalf of the controller.
