## Going deeper

A short list of durable resources for readers who want more than the lesson covers.

- **[Anthropic Privacy Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy).** The full privacy policy of the major AI vendor this lesson cites verbatim. Worth reading section 3 ("How We Disclose Personal Data") for the formal list of parties Anthropic discloses data to (affiliates and related entities; service providers and business partners; transactional disclosures during mergers or acquisitions; government and law-enforcement requests). The controller-versus-processor language about commercial customers is in the policy's preamble paragraphs (before section 1), where Anthropic explains it acts as a controller for its own services and as a processor for personal data it handles on behalf of commercial customers. The fourth part of this track returns to this policy as a worked example for the five-question vendor rubric.

- **[Apple Private Cloud Compute announcement](https://security.apple.com/blog/private-cloud-compute/).** The technical-overview blog post Apple Security Engineering published when PCC was introduced. Worth reading the "Designing Private Cloud Compute" section for the five core requirements (stateless computation, enforceable guarantees, no privileged runtime access, non-targetability, verifiable transparency) and how each one rules out a class of attack or insider access that traditional cloud AI services do not rule out. The fifth part of this track returns to PCC as one of the worked examples for architectural privacy.

- **[Clawless KB: "What is Under the Hood"](https://clawless.ai/docs/knowledge-base/whats-under-the-hood).** The chapter the previous lesson cited for the architectural-alternative paragraph. This lesson cites it more briefly, for the trust-boundaries framing ("what you are trusting to whom" with a short list of parties for a desktop AI app). Useful contrast reading: the party list for a desktop architecture is significantly shorter than the party list for a consumer-website architecture.

## Source limitations

These sources are good. Naming what each one is weaker on helps you use them well.

**Anthropic Privacy Policy.** This is one specific vendor's privacy policy, captured at a point in time. Vendor privacy policies revise on their own cadence (often quarterly, sometimes more frequently). The verbatim quotes this lesson uses (the safety-review language, the service-providers-and-business-partners enumeration, the controller-versus-processor language) are stable conceptually and appear in many vendors' policies in some form, but the exact wording is Anthropic's; if Anthropic restructures their policy substantively, the quotes may no longer appear in the form cited. The 6-month review cadence on this lesson is intended to catch that. The lesson uses Anthropic's wording because it is clear and stable, not because Anthropic's policy is exemplary or non-exemplary in any other respect (the fourth part of this track will evaluate vendor policies on their merits).

**Apple Private Cloud Compute announcement.** This is Apple's own engineering blog post about an Apple architecture. It is therefore an inherently self-interested document: Apple is making the case for their own design. The technical claims (stateless computation, no privileged runtime access, verifiable transparency) are specific and verifiable in principle through the public security-research process Apple committed to, but the document itself is not a neutral comparative survey of cloud AI architectures. Read it for what Apple specifically built and the requirements they set. Use it as one architectural data point, not as an industry overview.

**Clawless KB chapter "What is Under the Hood".** This is a sister-project knowledge-base chapter written by the Clawless team about Clawless. It is honest about Clawless's specific architecture. It is not a neutral comparative survey of all AI tool architectures. The chapter is the canonical worked example for one specific architectural shape (a desktop app that talks directly to the AI provider); it is not the only such shape. Read the chapter for the contrast case, not as a buyer's guide.
