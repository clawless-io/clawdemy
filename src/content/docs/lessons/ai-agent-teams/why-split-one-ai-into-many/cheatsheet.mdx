> For education only. Multi-agent architecture taught via stock analysis; not investment, financial, or trading advice.

## The one idea

A real agent team is one workflow decomposed into specialist roles by function. Spend your most capable model only at the judgment nodes (the points where one agent decides for the rest), and run everything else lean. (Pinned to TradingAgents commit 7e9e7b8.)

## The roster (twelve agents, by function)

| Cluster | Count | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Analysts | 4 | Gather and read raw information (market, social, news, fundamentals) |
| Researchers | 2 | Argue opposite sides: a bull and a bear |
| Trader | 1 | Turn the argument into a concrete plan |
| Risk seats | 3 | Stress-test the plan: aggressive, conservative, neutral |
| Managers | 2 | Judge: the research manager rules the debate, the portfolio manager makes the final call |

Twelve is the count with all four analysts on (the default). Analysts are selectable; the other eight roles are always built.

## Import order is not run order

Run-time pipeline: analysts gather, bull and bear argue, **research manager** rules, trader plans, three risk seats stress-test, **portfolio manager** decides. The two judges sit at two different points in the flow.

## The model-tier split

- Two tiers: a **deep** (more capable, costlier) model and a **quick** (cheaper, faster) one.
- Deep goes to exactly two agents: the **research manager** and the **portfolio manager** (the judges).
- The other ten run on quick.
- Invariant: change the analyst count and the headcount shifts, but the deep model always goes to exactly those two judges. That, not the number twelve, is the design.

## Build your own (the transferable pattern)

1. Decompose the workflow into functions; make each a role with narrow instructions and few tools.
2. Find the judgment nodes (where one agent commits for the rest) and put your best model only there. Expect far fewer of them than you would guess.

## Pitfalls to dodge

- Counting agents instead of functions (the count falls out of the decomposition; it is not the design).
- Using one model tier everywhere (slow and costly on top, unreliable at the judge).
- Assuming more roles means more intelligence (more specialized and more complex, not smarter).

## Words to use precisely

- **Factory:** a `create_*` function that returns one configured agent node, with its model injected as an argument; what makes a role replaceable.
- **Judgment node:** a point where one agent decides on behalf of the others; where you spend capability.
- **Model tier:** deep (capable, costly) versus quick (cheap, fast); assigned per role, not globally.
