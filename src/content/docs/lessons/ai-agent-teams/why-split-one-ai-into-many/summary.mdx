> For education only. This teaches multi-agent architecture using stock analysis as the example; it is not investment, financial, or trading advice.

**A real agent team is one workflow split into specialist roles by function, with your strongest model spent only where one agent decides for the rest.** This lesson reads the actual code of the open-source TradingAgents framework (pinned at commit 7e9e7b8) to make that concrete. This summary is the scan-in-five-minutes version of the full lesson.

## Core ideas

- **The roster is a decomposition, not a headcount.** The framework exports its agents grouped into functional clusters: four **analysts** who gather information, two **researchers** who argue opposite sides (a bull and a bear), one **trader** who turns the argument into a plan, three **risk seats** who stress-test it, and two **managers** who judge. The grouping follows the natural seams of one workflow.
- **Each role is a small, focused job.** Splitting buys focus (a narrow agent with a narrow toolset is more reliable than a generalist), deliberate opposition (separate bull and bear agents argue at full strength instead of one model talking itself into a tidy answer), and replaceability (each role is its own factory you can swap without touching the others).
- **Import order is not run order.** At run time the pipeline goes analysts, then the bull and bear debate, then the **research manager** rules, then the trader plans, then the three risk seats stress-test, then the **portfolio manager** makes the final call. The two judges sit at two different points in the flow.
- **The decision most people miss: spend capability at the judgment nodes.** The system runs on two model tiers, a more capable "deep" model and a cheaper "quick" one, and it gives the deep model to exactly the two judges (the research manager and the portfolio manager). Everything else runs on the quick model. Change how many analysts you run and the headcount shifts, but the deep model still goes to exactly those two judges. That invariant, not the number twelve, is the design.

## What changes for you

Before this lesson, "split one AI into many" was a slogan. Now you can read a real production agent team and see the two decisions underneath it: decompose the workflow into roles by function, then find the small number of points where one agent commits on behalf of the rest and put your best model only there. That is a pattern you can carry into any agent system you design, in any domain.
