## Primary source

**Dan Hendrycks. *Introduction to AI Safety, Ethics, and Society*. Taylor & Francis, 2024.** Center for AI Safety, free to read at [aisafetybook.com](https://www.aisafetybook.com/). Sections 8.2 (Growth) and 8.3 (Distribution) inform the broader economic framing the lesson does not work in detail.

## Verbatim quotes used in the lesson

A1 discipline preserved: verbatim from cited sections, no paraphrasing inside quote marks.

- **§8.6 International Governance, core framing:** *"international cooperation is important in order to manage risks from AI."*
- **§8.6 International Governance, on nuclear-precedent parallel:** *"offense-dominant"* (used by the chapter to describe both AI and nuclear weapons in the context of agreement-deviation risk).
- **§8.6 International Governance, on aviation-certification analogy:** *"require domestic regulators to have certain verification procedures"* (the §8.6 review-question answer reads "International aviation safety standards require domestic regulators to have certain verification procedures"; it is followed by the point that nations comply to maintain access to others' airspace).
- **§8.7 Compute Governance, on the lever:** *"Compute is indispensable for developing and deploying AIs. Restricting access to compute allows control over what AIs are created and used."*
- **§8.7 Compute Governance, on tractability:** *"Compute is physical, excludable, and quantifiable which allows it to be tracked, restricted, and measured."*

The four-layer taxonomy itself (corporate, national, international, compute) is the chapter's organizing structure; the specific mechanisms named within each layer (RSPs, model cards, EU AI Act provisions, IAEA-style organizations, FLOP-based thresholds) are drawn from the broader AI-policy literature that Ch 8 reflects.

## Suggested companion reading

Not required for L9; these are the major works in current AI-policy and compute-governance discussion.

- **EU AI Act:** the full text is at [eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689). The Future of Life Institute maintains an accessible structured summary at [artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/). Worth reading for the specific instruments (the high-risk categorization, the general-purpose AI obligations, the systemic-risk threshold in Article 51) the lesson references. Status as of 2026-11-16: the Act entered into force on 1 August 2024; the general-purpose AI obligations have applied since 2 August 2025, and the Commission's enforcement powers for them apply from 2 August 2026 ([Commission page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-gpai-providers)). The Digital Omnibus on AI, [Regulation (EU) 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32026R1744) (published 24 July 2026, in force 27 July 2026), moved the high-risk obligations for Annex III systems to 2 December 2027 and for Annex I systems to 2 August 2028.
- **US Executive Order on AI (2023):** EO 14110 as published, 88 FR 75191, is in the [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/11/01/2023-24283/safe-secure-and-trustworthy-development-and-use-of-artificial-intelligence). The same entry records that it was revoked by EO 14148 on January 20, 2025, so read it as policy history rather than as current law. Later US executive orders on AI include [EO 14365](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/16/2025-23092/ensuring-a-national-policy-framework-for-artificial-intelligence), "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence" (signed 11 December 2025; 90 FR 58499), which concerns state AI laws. For policy-context analysis, [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/) is a good entry point.
- **NIST AI Risk Management Framework:** the official document at [airc.nist.gov/AI_RMF_Knowledge_Base](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/). AI RMF 1.0 (NIST AI 100-1) was released on January 26, 2023 as a voluntary framework; [NIST's own page](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) states it is being revised as part of the White House AI Action Plan (page checked 2026-11-16), and lists a Generative AI Profile (NIST-AI-600-1, July 26, 2024).
- **Compute governance:** Girish Sastry, Lennart Heim, Haydn Belfield, Markus Anderljung, et al., *"Computing Power and the Governance of Artificial Intelligence"* (arXiv preprint, submitted February 13, 2024), at [arxiv.org/abs/2402.08797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08797). A widely cited treatment of compute as a governance lever; the lesson's discussion of compute's physical-excludable-quantifiable properties is anchored here.
- **Responsible Scaling Policies:** Anthropic's RSP at [anthropic.com/responsible-scaling-policy](https://www.anthropic.com/responsible-scaling-policy) (Version 3.4, effective July 8, 2026, page last updated August 14, 2026; Version 3.0 of February 24, 2026 was a full rewrite, and the page states that Anthropic remains free to pause development in circumstances it deems appropriate) and METR's [Common Elements of Frontier AI Safety Policies](https://metr.org/common-elements) (December 16, 2025), which compares the published policies of twelve developers. Worth reading both to see corporate-layer commitments in their current form and an outside comparison of how they are built.
- **Nuclear-AI analogy:** Zaidi and Dafoe, *"International Control of Powerful Technology: Lessons from the Baruch Plan for Nuclear Weapons"* (GovAI, 2021), at [cdn.governance.ai](https://cdn.governance.ai/International-Control-of-Powerful-Technology-Lessons-from-the-Baruch-Plan-Zaidi-Dafoe-2021.pdf). A historical case study of an attempt at international control of a dangerous technology, with lessons the authors apply to AI.
- **Bletchley Declaration (2023) and successor summit documents:** the [Frontier AI Safety Commitments](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/frontier-ai-safety-commitments-ai-seoul-summit-2024/frontier-ai-safety-commitments-ai-seoul-summit-2024) (AI Seoul Summit 2024, GOV.UK page updated 7 February 2025) are voluntary pledges by developers to publish safety frameworks and set risk thresholds. The Bletchley declaration text is at [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-safety-summit-2023-the-bletchley-declaration/the-bletchley-declaration-by-countries-attending-the-ai-safety-summit-1-2-november-2023). Worth reading as a primary-source example of a multilateral norm-development instrument (international governance without binding-treaty teeth). The UK institute created after Bletchley was renamed the AI Security Institute on 14 February 2025 ([GOV.UK announcement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/tackling-ai-security-risks-to-unleash-growth-and-deliver-plan-for-change)).
