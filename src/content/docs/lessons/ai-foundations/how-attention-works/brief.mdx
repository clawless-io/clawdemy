## What you'll learn

This is the opener of Phase 2, *How models think: the transformer architecture*, in Track 5 (AI Foundations). The Stanford CME 295 course materials (syllabus, schedule, the Amidi cheatsheets) are at [cme295.stanford.edu](https://cme295.stanford.edu/). Phase 1 left you with a sequence of dense vectors, one per token, ready to flow into the model. This lesson is the mechanism that turns those vectors into a model that knows which words go with which: self-attention. It answers one question: when an AI reads a sentence, how does it figure out which words go with which? We're going to build the intuition without burying you in linear algebra, work through one attention score by hand, and stop before the math swallows the meaning.

## Where this fits

This is lesson 1 of Phase 2, *How models think: the transformer architecture*, and the Phase 2 opener. Phase 1 traced a sentence from raw text through tokens and embeddings into a sequence of dense vectors. This lesson covers what the model does with those vectors: the attention mechanism. The next lesson is *Multi-head attention*, which extends attention from one computation to many running in parallel.

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** the Phase 1 lessons, especially [How AI reads tokens](/lessons/ai-foundations/how-ai-reads-tokens/lesson/) and [Embeddings](/lessons/ai-foundations/how-words-become-vectors/lesson/). This lesson assumes you know what a token ID is and what an embedding vector represents. You don't need prior ML background beyond that. If you're rusty on what a dot product does, watch [3Blue1Brown's "Dot products and duality"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LyGKycYT2v0) (about 14 minutes) before you start. It's the one piece of math intuition the lesson assumes; everything else is explained inline.

## By the end, you'll be able to

- Explain in plain language what attention does and why it replaced the sequence-by-sequence approach RNNs used
- Distinguish self-attention from cross-attention by what each one is comparing
- Decompose the attention formula into the role each of its three inputs (query, key, value) plays in producing one score
- Run the attention computation by hand on a small worked matrix of three tokens
