## What you'll learn

This is lesson 5 of Phase 2, *How models think: the transformer architecture*, in Track 5 (AI Foundations). Previous lessons covered the architectural changes the field made after 2017: normalization schemes and their placement. This lesson covers the third area of change: attention efficiency. Specifically, two distinct problems with two distinct fixes. The first is the **compute problem**: standard self-attention is O(n^2) in sequence length, which becomes a bottleneck at the long contexts modern LLMs run on. The fix is **sliding window attention**: each token attends only to its local neighborhood. The second is the **memory problem**: the KV cache that makes decoding fast also gets large fast. The fix is **sharing key and value projections across attention heads**, in a progression from multi-head attention (MHA) through multi-query attention (MQA) to group-query attention (GQA). The lecture treats these as two orthogonal efficiency moves; this lesson keeps them cleanly separate. Course materials are at [cme295.stanford.edu](https://cme295.stanford.edu/).

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** the [multi-head attention lesson](/lessons/ai-foundations/multi-head-attention/lesson/) and the [transformer block lesson](/lessons/ai-foundations/transformer-block/lesson/) are required. We assume you understand what an attention head is, what `Q`, `K`, `V` projection matrices are, and what the attention computation looks like. If those terms feel unfamiliar, read the corresponding lesson first.

## By the end, you'll be able to

- Explain why standard self-attention is O(n^2) in compute and where that becomes a real bottleneck
- Describe sliding window attention and the receptive-field analogy that explains how local layers still see distant tokens through stacking
- Distinguish the compute problem (attention complexity) from the memory problem (KV cache size during decoding); explain why they need different fixes
- Walk through the MHA -> MQA -> GQA progression and explain why most modern LLMs land on GQA
