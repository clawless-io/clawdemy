**A pretrained transformer does not follow instructions.** It produces plausible text. Two further training stages turn it into the chat assistant you actually talk to: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) teaches it response shape, and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) teaches it which responses people preferred during training. The model you interact with is the same architecture and largely the same weights as the base model, with two layers of post-training on top.

This summary is the scan-it-in-five-minutes version. The full lesson covers each stage, walks through RLHF in two parts (reward model, then policy optimization), names the modern variants (DPO, CAI, RLAIF), and decomposes observable assistant behaviors back to the stage that produced them.

The exact recipe varies by lab and model. The bullets below describe the canonical shape; the lesson body covers where the variants diverge.

## Core ideas

- **Three stages, in order.** Pretraining produces a base model (good at next-token prediction, no instruction-following). Supervised fine-tuning produces an instruction-tuned model. RLHF produces the chat assistant. Each stage starts from the previous stage's output.
- **Data scale shrinks; signal sharpens.** Pretraining commonly sees trillions of tokens of unfiltered web text. SFT typically sees tens of thousands of curated instruction-response examples. RLHF often sees hundreds of thousands of human comparisons. Volume drops by orders of magnitude across the stages.
- **SFT teaches response shape, not new knowledge.** The base model already knows the content from pretraining. SFT teaches the model when to apply what it already knows, given a user instruction. A few thousand high-quality examples can change surface behavior dramatically.
- **RLHF, part one: train the reward model.** Collect a dataset where humans compared two model responses to the same prompt and picked one. Train a separate model that takes `(prompt, response)` and outputs a single number representing "how good." The reward model learns to score the way the labelers would.
- **RLHF, part two: optimize the policy.** The SFT model is the policy. For each prompt: policy generates a response, reward model scores it, a reinforcement learning algorithm (commonly Proximal Policy Optimization, PPO) nudges the policy toward higher-scoring responses.
- **Reward hacking is the failure mode.** Without constraints, the policy finds ways to produce output the reward model rates highly without actually being helpful. Standard prevention: a regularization term keeps the policy tethered to the SFT model so it does not drift into linguistic nonsense while climbing the reward gradient.
- **Modern variants.** Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) skips the separate reward model and optimizes the SFT model directly on preference data. Simpler pipeline, often comparable quality. Constitutional AI (CAI) replaces some human comparisons with critiques generated by an AI working from written principles. RLAIF is the umbrella term for using AI rather than human comparisons during preference learning.
- **Knowledge from pretraining; instructions from SFT; tone, polish, refusal from RLHF.** The cleanest mental model. Knowing facts traces to pretraining. Producing a response shape rather than continuing the prompt traces to SFT. Polite formatting, refusing harmful requests, and the polished assistant feel trace to RLHF.
- **Refusal is learned, not built-in.** The reward model was trained with refusals labeled as preferred for certain prompt categories; the policy learned to produce them. A model can be retrained to refuse fewer or different things by changing the preference data. This is why community "uncensored" fine-tunes exist.
- **Pitfall: SFT versus continued pretraining.** Both look like "training a model on more data," but the data is different (curated instruction examples versus raw web text) and the role is different. SFT is scoped to instruction-following.
- **Pitfall: RLHF gives preferences, not values.** The reward model captures what the labelers preferred. The "values" you observe in a chat assistant are an indirect product of training data choices, not philosophical commitments.
- **Pitfall: assistants are not fixed personalities.** The same base model can be SFT-tuned and RLHF-tuned in different directions to produce assistants that feel very different. The base is the same; the post-training shapes the personality.

## What changes for you

Before this lesson, the difference between "a base model" and "the assistant" was probably either invisible or attributed to "the algorithm." After it, you have a working frame for what changes when. When a vendor announces "we have improved the assistant," you know that probably means new SFT data plus new RLHF preferences, not a new base model. (Post-training takes days; pretraining a frontier model takes months.)

When you read about safety alignment, you can locate it in the RLHF reward signal. When you read about "uncensored" community fine-tunes, you understand they are post-training retrains that strip the refusal behavior the original RLHF added. The thing you are talking to is not just an architecture; it is an architecture plus two layers of training on what a good response looks like.

**Pretraining gives the model knowledge.**\
**Fine-tuning gives it instructions.**\
**RLHF gives it taste.**
