## What you'll learn

This is lesson 5 of Phase 2, *How models think: the transformer architecture*, in Track 5 (AI Foundations). The previous lesson assembled everything into a complete transformer block, including the "Add & Norm" boxes that appear in the original architecture diagram. This lesson zooms in on those boxes: what normalization actually does, and the two ways the field has changed how it works since 2017. The first change is **where** the normalization sits relative to the sub-layer (post-norm to pre-norm). The second change is **what** the normalization computes (LayerNorm to RMSNorm). Both show up in modern model cards. Course materials are at [cme295.stanford.edu](https://cme295.stanford.edu/).

## Where this fits

This is lesson 5 of Phase 2, *How models think: the transformer architecture*. The previous lesson ([How modern models inject position into attention (RoPE)](/lessons/ai-foundations/position-embeddings-and-rope/lesson/)) covered the structural shift from input-added to attention-injected position embeddings. This lesson gives normalization its full treatment. The next lesson, *How transformers scale to real-world data: sliding windows and KV-cache savings*, covers the attention efficiency improvements that make long-context inference tractable.

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** the [transformer block lesson](/lessons/ai-foundations/transformer-block/lesson/) is required. We assume you understand what a sub-layer is in the transformer (attention or feed-forward network), what residual connections do, and what the "Add & Norm" box represents in the original architecture diagram. If those terms feel unfamiliar, read the transformer block lesson first.

## By the end, you'll be able to

- Explain in plain language what layer normalization does to a vector and why it helps training
- Distinguish LayerNorm from BatchNorm and explain why transformers use the former
- Identify the structural shift from post-norm to pre-norm and the reason modern transformers use pre-norm
- Describe how RMSNorm differs from LayerNorm and why most modern LLMs use it

- **Practice time:** about 12 minutes (a worked LayerNorm computation by hand on a small vector, plus a comparison exercise on the post-norm vs pre-norm equations)
