## What you'll learn

This is the second lesson in our adaptation of Stanford CME 295 Lecture 2 (Transformer-based models & tricks). The previous lesson covered position embeddings and the modern shift to RoPE. This one covers normalization: what those small "Add & Norm" boxes in the original transformer diagram actually do, and the two ways the field has changed how they work since 2017. The first change is **where** the normalization sits relative to the sub-layer (post-norm to pre-norm). The second change is **what** the normalization computes (LayerNorm to RMSNorm). Both show up in modern model cards. Course materials are at [cme295.stanford.edu](https://cme295.stanford.edu/).

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** the transformer block lesson (in our Lecture 1 adaptation) is required reading. We assume you understand what a sub-layer is in the transformer (attention or feed-forward network), what residual connections do, and what an "Add & Norm" box looks like in the original architecture diagram. The position embeddings + RoPE lesson (the previous one in this lecture) is helpful context but not required; that lesson and this one cover independent parts of the modern transformer's evolution from the 2017 paper.

## By the end, you'll be able to

- Explain in plain language what layer normalization does to a vector and why it helps training
- Distinguish LayerNorm from BatchNorm and explain why transformers use the former
- Identify the structural shift from post-norm to pre-norm and the reason modern transformers use pre-norm
- Describe how RMSNorm differs from LayerNorm and why most modern LLMs use it

## Time and difficulty

- **Read time:** about 18 minutes
- **Practice time:** about 12 minutes (a worked LayerNorm computation by hand on a small vector, plus a comparison exercise on the post-norm vs pre-norm equations)
- **Difficulty:** standard
