## What you'll learn

This is the fifth and final lesson in our adaptation of Stanford CME 295: Transformers & Large Language Models, Lecture 1. The full course materials are at [cme295.stanford.edu](https://cme295.stanford.edu/). By the end of lessons 1 through 4 you have seen tokens, embeddings, attention, and multi-head attention. Every load-bearing piece. But a real transformer is not just stacked attention layers; it is stacked **blocks**, and a block is more than attention. This lesson covers the four pieces that wrap around attention (position encoding, feed-forward network, residual connections, layer normalization), why each one is necessary, and what the architecture diagram in the original "Attention Is All You Need" paper actually represents. Once you finish this lesson, you can read the original transformer architecture diagram and name every component, and you have the vocabulary to recognize the variants modern models build on top of it (RMSNorm, RoPE, grouped-query attention, mixture of experts, and so on).

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** Lessons 1 and 4 are strongly recommended. Lesson 1 ("Inside the transformer: how attention works") gave you the single-head mechanism; Lesson 4 ("Multi-head attention") generalized it. This lesson assumes both are familiar. Lesson 3 ("Embeddings") is also useful since position encoding modifies the embedding output before the first block runs; if any of those terms are unfamiliar, read the corresponding lesson first.

## By the end, you'll be able to

- Identify the four pieces that wrap around attention to make a complete transformer block (position encoding, feed-forward network, residual connections, layer normalization)
- Explain why attention alone is not enough and what each wrapping piece adds
- Walk through a single forward pass through one block, in order, and trace what changes at each step
- Read the "Attention Is All You Need" architecture diagram and name every component on it

## Time and difficulty

- **Read time:** about 25 minutes
- **Practice time:** about 15 minutes (a forward-pass trace by hand, plus annotating the canonical architecture diagram)
- **Difficulty:** standard
