## What you'll learn

This is the second lesson in our adaptation of Stanford CME 295: Transformers & Large Language Models, Lecture 1. The full course materials (syllabus, schedule, the Amidi cheatsheets) are at [cme295.stanford.edu](https://cme295.stanford.edu/). In lesson 1 we built the heart of the transformer: self-attention. We waved at where its inputs came from, just saying "each token is a vector". This lesson is what those tokens actually are, and how raw text gets turned into them. We'll build the intuition without algorithm overload, walk through one byte-pair encoding merge by hand, and end with the demonstration that explains why GPT can't count the Rs in "strawberry".

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** none from Clawdemy yet. Lesson 1 ("Inside the transformer: how attention works") is a useful companion but not required. If you have never seen the word "vocabulary" used in the AI sense, this lesson defines it as we go.

## By the end, you'll be able to

- Explain in plain language why AI models read tokens, not whole words or individual letters
- Identify the tradeoff between vocabulary size and sequence length that tokenization is designed to manage
- Walk through one byte-pair encoding (BPE) merge by hand on a small input
- Predict what a real tokenizer will output for a given string before checking, including the failure mode that explains why GPT miscounts the Rs in "strawberry"

## Time and difficulty

- **Read time:** about 20 minutes
- **Practice time:** about 15 minutes (predict-and-check using tiktokenizer, plus flashcards)
- **Difficulty:** standard
