## What you'll learn

This is the opener of Phase 1, *How models read text*, in Track 5 (AI Foundations). The Stanford CME 295 course materials (syllabus, schedule, the Amidi cheatsheets) are at [cme295.stanford.edu](https://cme295.stanford.edu/). A transformer never sees raw text directly. It sees a sequence of integer IDs called *tokens*. This lesson is what those tokens actually are, and how raw text gets turned into them. We'll build the intuition without algorithm overload, walk through one byte-pair encoding merge by hand, and end with the demonstration that explains why some tokenizers can't count the Rs in "strawberry".

## Where this fits

This is lesson 1 of Phase 1, *How models read text*, and the Track 5 entry point. There is no previous lesson in the track. Phase 1 ends when you can trace a sentence from raw input through tokens, embeddings, and positional information into the sequence of dense vectors the model actually operates on; this lesson covers the first step. The next lesson is *Embeddings: how words become vectors with meaning*, which picks up directly where this one leaves off.

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** none. This is the Track 5 entry point. If you have never seen the word "vocabulary" used in the AI sense, this lesson defines it as we go.

## By the end, you'll be able to

- Explain in plain language why AI models read tokens, not whole words or individual letters
- Identify the tradeoff between vocabulary size and sequence length that tokenization is designed to manage
- Walk through one byte-pair encoding (BPE) merge by hand on a small input
- Predict what a real tokenizer will output for a given string before checking, including the failure mode that explains why GPT miscounts the Rs in "strawberry"

- **Practice time:** about 15 minutes (predict-and-check using tiktokenizer, plus flashcards)
