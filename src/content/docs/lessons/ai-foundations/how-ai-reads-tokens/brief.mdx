## What you'll learn

This is the first lesson in our adaptation of Stanford CME 295 Lecture 1 (Transformer). The full course materials (syllabus, schedule, the Amidi cheatsheets) are at [cme295.stanford.edu](https://cme295.stanford.edu/). A transformer never sees raw text directly. It sees a sequence of integer IDs called *tokens*. This lesson is what those tokens actually are, and how raw text gets turned into them. We'll build the intuition without algorithm overload, walk through one byte-pair encoding merge by hand, and end with the demonstration that explains why some tokenizers can't count the Rs in "strawberry".

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** none from Clawdemy yet. The attention lesson (later in this lecture) builds on the tokens we cover here, but you can read either one first standalone. If you have never seen the word "vocabulary" used in the AI sense, this lesson defines it as we go.

## By the end, you'll be able to

- Explain in plain language why AI models read tokens, not whole words or individual letters
- Identify the tradeoff between vocabulary size and sequence length that tokenization is designed to manage
- Walk through one byte-pair encoding (BPE) merge by hand on a small input
- Predict what a real tokenizer will output for a given string before checking, including the failure mode that explains why GPT miscounts the Rs in "strawberry"

## Time and difficulty

- **Read time:** about 20 minutes
- **Practice time:** about 15 minutes (predict-and-check using tiktokenizer, plus flashcards)
- **Difficulty:** standard
