## What you'll learn

This is the fifth lesson in our adaptation of Stanford CME 295 Lecture 2 (Transformer-based models & tricks). The previous lesson covered the T5 family (encoder-decoder + span corruption). This lesson covers the encoder-only branch of the architectural tree: **BERT** (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers). BERT drops the decoder entirely, makes self-attention bidirectional by removing the causal mask, and pretrains via two new objectives that take advantage of bidirectionality: **masked language model (MLM)** and **next sentence prediction (NSP)**. The result is an encoder that produces context-aware token representations useful for classification and span-detection tasks. We'll walk the architecture, the structural tokens (CLS, SEP) and the new segment encoding, both pretraining objectives in detail, and the train-then-fine-tune workflow that defines how BERT-family models get used. Course materials are at [cme295.stanford.edu](https://cme295.stanford.edu/).

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** the transformer block lesson and the multi-head attention lesson (in our Lecture 1 adaptation) are required reading. We assume you understand what an encoder is, what causal masking does to attention, and what an embedding lookup looks like. The text generation lesson and the previous L2.4 (encoder-decoder + T5) lesson are useful context but not required.

## By the end, you'll be able to

- Explain what makes BERT bidirectional and how that contrasts with the causal masking in decoder-only models
- Identify the role of the structural tokens (CLS, SEP) and the segment encoding in BERT's input
- Walk through the two pre-training objectives (MLM with its 80/10/10 masking mix, and NSP) and what each one teaches the model
- Describe the multi-stage train-then-fine-tune workflow and the typical fine-tuning patterns (CLS classification head, per-token tasks like question-answering span detection)

## Time and difficulty

- **Read time:** about 26 minutes
- **Practice time:** about 18 minutes (a walked-example tokenization through the BERT input pipeline plus a fine-tuning pattern matching exercise across sentiment, question-answering, and token-classification tasks)
- **Difficulty:** standard
