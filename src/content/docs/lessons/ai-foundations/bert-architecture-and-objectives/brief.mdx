## What you'll learn

This is lesson 8 of Phase 2, *How models think: the transformer architecture*, in Track 5 (AI Foundations). The previous lesson covered the encoder-decoder branch of the architectural tree (T5 and span corruption). This lesson covers the encoder-only branch: **BERT** (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers). BERT drops the decoder entirely, makes self-attention bidirectional by removing the causal mask, and pretrains via two new objectives that take advantage of bidirectionality: **masked language model (MLM)** and **next sentence prediction (NSP)**. The result is an encoder that produces context-aware token representations useful for classification and span-detection tasks. We'll walk the architecture, the structural tokens (CLS, SEP) and the new segment encoding, both pretraining objectives in detail, and the train-then-fine-tune workflow that defines how BERT-family models get used. Course materials are at [cme295.stanford.edu](https://cme295.stanford.edu/).

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** the [transformer block lesson](/lessons/ai-foundations/transformer-block/lesson/), the [multi-head attention lesson](/lessons/ai-foundations/multi-head-attention/lesson/), and the [encoder-decoder and T5 lesson](/lessons/ai-foundations/encoder-decoder-and-t5-span-corruption/lesson/) are required. We assume you understand what an encoder is, what causal masking does to attention, and what an embedding lookup looks like.

## By the end, you'll be able to

- Explain what makes BERT bidirectional and how that contrasts with the causal masking in decoder-only models
- Identify the role of the structural tokens (CLS, SEP) and the segment encoding in BERT's input
- Walk through the two pre-training objectives (MLM with its 80/10/10 masking mix, and NSP) and what each one teaches the model
- Describe the multi-stage train-then-fine-tune workflow and the typical fine-tuning patterns (CLS classification head, per-token tasks like question-answering span detection)
