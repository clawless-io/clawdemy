## What you'll learn

This is the first lesson in our adaptation of Stanford CME 295 Lecture 3 (Large Language Models). Lecture 1 (the previous five lessons in this track) built the transformer architecture: tokens, embeddings, attention, multi-head attention, the full transformer block. Lecture 3 picks up at how that trained architecture actually does its job at runtime. The model does not produce text in one shot. It produces text one token at a time, in a loop: forward pass through every block, softmax over the vocabulary, sample one token, append it to the input, run the forward pass again. Decoding strategies (greedy, sampling, top-k, top-p, temperature) shape that sampling step in different ways and often produce noticeably different output styles. We'll walk the loop, compare the strategies, and explain what temperature does. The full course materials are at [cme295.stanford.edu](https://cme295.stanford.edu/).

## Before you start

**Prerequisites:** the attention and transformer block lessons (Lecture 1) are strongly recommended. We assume you understand what a transformer block is and what it produces at the top of the stack. The tokens and embeddings lessons are useful supporting context but not required. If "logits" or "softmax" feel unfamiliar, the attention lesson covers softmax at the depth this lesson assumes.

## By the end, you'll be able to

- Explain how a transformer produces text autoregressively (one token at a time) and trace the prediction loop end to end
- Compare the most common decoding strategies (greedy, sampling, top-k, top-p) and name the trade-off each one makes
- Explain what temperature does to the probability distribution and how it shapes the model's output style
- Explain what KV caching is and why generation gets faster after the first token

## Time and difficulty

- **Read time:** about 22 minutes
- **Practice time:** about 15 minutes (sampling-by-hand on a tiny logits vector, plus a quick decoding-strategy comparison on a hosted playground)
- **Difficulty:** standard
