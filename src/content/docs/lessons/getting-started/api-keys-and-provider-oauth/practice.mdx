## Self-check

Six quick questions. Answer each in your head before opening the collapsible.

**1. What does BYOK stand for, and what does it actually mean for who bills you?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

BYOK stands for "bring your own key." It means the API key represents your account with the AI provider, not your account with [Clawless Computer](https://clawless.ai). The provider bills you directly for every message at the provider's published rates; Clawless Computer takes no markup and does not sit in the middle of the bill.

</details>

**2. Where does Clawless Computer store the API key after you click Save?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

On your computer. OpenClaw, the open-source engine Clawless Computer runs on, keeps the key in its own database inside your Clawless Computer data folder, readable only by your user account. It is not encrypted there, so turning on your computer's disk encryption (FileVault on a Mac, BitLocker on Windows) adds protection. Clawless Computer never displays a saved key.

</details>

**3. Where in Clawless Computer do you add or remove a key after onboarding?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

Open Settings from the gear icon at the bottom of the navigation rail on the far left, then click the API Keys section in the sticky sidebar on the left of the Settings page. Every supported provider appears as a row with its current status; click a row to add or change a key.

</details>

**4. You connected Anthropic first, then added Google, and now you want new chats to start on a Google model. Where do you change that, and what happens to chats you already have?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

Open Settings, API Keys, and click the Default model for new chats button. Pick the Google model and press Make default. New chats start with it, and so do agents that have no model of their own. Chats you already have keep their model, and a model you pick inside one chat changes only that chat. (If you wanted an OpenAI model instead: OpenAI works with an API key; signing in with a ChatGPT account comes back in a later update.)

</details>

**5. A provider's row in Settings, API Keys turned red this afternoon after working fine this morning. Name the three most common causes.**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

Typo on paste (re-copy and save again), the key was revoked or rotated on the provider side (generate a new one), or the account is out of credits (top up on the provider's site). The row in Clawless Computer shows a red message; read it as your first clue.

</details>

**6. You install Clawless Computer on a second computer. Are your keys already there?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

No. Keys do not sync between computers; each install keeps its own data on that machine. You either add your keys again, or move your whole data folder from the old computer, which brings your keys with it. Because the data folder holds your keys, keep any backup copy of it somewhere only you can reach.

</details>

## Try it yourself: the 15-minute key check

The body lesson covered the theory. This is the short hands-on. The goal is to confirm your existing provider is healthy, and optionally to add a second so you have a fallback. Fifteen minutes if you already have a key from another provider handy. Longer if you have to sign up for one.

**Side effects:** small. Adding a second provider does not change your existing chats. The one test message in step 6 uses a little of that provider's free allowance or credit.

**Steps:**

1. **Open Settings.** Click the gear icon at the bottom of the navigation rail on the far left of the Clawless Computer window. The Settings page opens.

2. **Click the API Keys section** in the sticky sidebar on the left of the Settings page.

3. **Confirm your current provider is green.** Find the row for the provider you connected during onboarding. Look for the green check that means the key authenticates and the account is reachable. If the row is red, read the brief description and fix the underlying cause (re-copy the key, generate a new one, or top up credits on the provider's site).

4. **Optional: add a second provider.** Pick one you do not have yet. A free-tier-friendly provider is a gentle choice (Google's Gemini free tier or Groq) so you can experiment without spending. Click the row, click the Get API Key link, generate a key on the provider's site, copy it, paste it into the Clawless Computer input box, click Save. A green check appears when Clawless Computer verifies it.

5. **Confirm the new provider appears in the model picker.** Go back to the chat. Click the model chip just above the message box. The picker lists models only from providers that hold a key, so you should now see the new provider's models, each with the provider named next to it.

6. **Send one message on the new provider.** Pick a small model from the new provider's list. Send "Tell me one short fact about the Roman aqueducts" or any quick question. Confirm the reply lands, then open the Usage dashboard (Usage, in the navigation rail) and find the request under the model you used.

**Expected outcome:** you finish with at least one green provider row that is verified working, the path to add or remove providers is in your hands, and you have seen what a healthy versus broken row looks like before it bites you in the middle of a real conversation.

If something went sideways: the five first-day surprises at the end of the lesson body cover the most common confusions, especially the "you do not pay Clawless Computer for AI usage" and "free tiers vary widely" ones.

<Flashcard question="What happens to your past conversations when you remove a provider's key?">

Nothing. Past conversations stay in your local conversation history regardless of whether the key that produced them is still saved. Keys are about authentication going forward, not about access to your history.

</Flashcard>

<Flashcard question="What does the green check on a provider row mean (and not mean)?">

It means the key authenticates and the account is reachable. It does not mean the model is fast today, that the provider has no rate limits, or that your free credits have not run out. Those are runtime conditions you discover as you go.

</Flashcard>
