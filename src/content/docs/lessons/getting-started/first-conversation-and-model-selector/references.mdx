## Source material

The lesson is anchored on four chapters from the [Clawless Computer](https://clawless.ai) knowledge base (Clawless Computer documentation, RBJ Global LLC), the single source of truth for Clawless Computer behavior. Inside the app, open Settings, About, and click Documentation to read these help chapters. The product itself lives at [clawless.ai](https://clawless.ai).

## Going deeper inside Clawless Computer

Three KB chapters to read in full once you have the basics down. The last two are also sources for this lesson.

- **Clawless Computer KB: "Agents and Overview"**. The full tour of the agent rail you met in this lesson. Each agent's purpose, what makes Assistant the default, and when to reach for which.

- **Clawless Computer KB: "Usage and Cost"**. The Usage panel in detail, including the breakdown by model and by session you opened at the end of the practice. Worth reading after your first week of conversations.

- **Clawless Computer KB: "Settings Reference"**. Every Settings category, including the "Default model for new chats" setting under API Keys (the model new chats start with, rather than a per-conversation pick in the picker).

## Going deeper outside Clawless Computer

Two outside sources for readers who want context on why model choice matters at all.

- **["Choosing the right model"](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/choosing-a-model)** Anthropic's own framing of when Opus, Sonnet, or Haiku is the right call. The article is provider-specific but the *shape* of the question (capability vs speed vs cost) generalizes to every provider in the Clawless Computer picker. Read it once, then make your own version of the decision tree as you use different models on real work.

- **["Pricing"](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)** at OpenAI. The actual per-token cost numbers for OpenAI's current model lineup. The dollar amounts move and the table changes shape, but reading it once gives you a calibration anchor: "the small model is roughly N times cheaper than the big one." That ratio holds across most providers.
