## Self-check

Six quick questions. Answer each in your head before opening the collapsible.

**1. What is the CostGuard mental model in one sentence?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

CostGuard is a software safety net you configure once that tracks your BYOK spending against a monthly cap and either warns or blocks at the threshold you pick, so a runaway agent loop or an experimental prompt is caught at your next message instead of running on unnoticed. With Block on (the default), it can stop what [Clawless Computer](https://clawless.ai) itself starts; channel replies and scheduled jobs count toward the total but are not stopped by it.

</details>

**2. Which usage counts toward your CostGuard cap, and which does not?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Counts:** pay-as-you-go API keys (Anthropic, Google AI, Groq, OpenAI, and most other providers; the cost of every message adds up). **Does not count:** local models (free, shown as "Local, free"), models whose price is not known (shown as "Price not known"), and image generation. The last two are not counted, so the cap cannot stop what they spend, and your provider still bills you for them.

</details>

**3. You hit 100% of your cap and the next send is rejected. What are the two unblock paths?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

Raise the cap in Settings, Budget (which unblocks immediately on save), or wait for the period to reset (the default is a rolling 30-day window, so the oldest day's spend ages out today; alternatively switch to calendar-month resets in Settings). A red banner shows on the home screen at 100%; with Block on, the banner when a send is stopped reads "Monthly budget reached. Bump your cap in Settings to keep going."

</details>

**4. Trace the data path of a single message from your keyboard to the agent's reply. How many servers does the conversation content touch?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

The path is: your keyboard, the Clawless Computer app on your computer, the bundled OpenClaw engine (still on your computer), the AI provider over the internet, OpenClaw, Clawless Computer, your screen. The conversation content touches **one server**: the AI provider's. There is no Clawless Computer server in the data path, and there is no license server or account. (If you connect a messaging channel or your agent uses web search, those services are contacted too, on your behalf.)

</details>

**5. What survives, what stops working, and what stays available offline if Clawless Computer went out of business tomorrow?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

**Survives:** your conversations, memories, settings, agent definitions, and API keys, all in one data folder on your computer. They are not in any Clawless Computer cloud, so they do not disappear when the company does. **Stops:** updates from us. There is no license server and no account, so nothing locks you out. **Stays available offline:** local models. The app launches and runs offline, and with a local model you can chat with no internet, which doubles as the answer for "what if I am on a plane" and "what if I need a setup that depends on no one else's servers."

</details>

**6. Your cap is reached and Block is on, but a scheduled job keeps running and a Telegram channel keeps replying. Bug or feature?**

<details>
<summary>Show answer</summary>

Expected behavior. Channel replies and scheduled jobs running on their schedule run inside the engine without going through Clawless Computer. What they spend counts toward your total, so you reach the cap sooner, but the cap cannot stop them while they run. Pause them yourself when you get close to your cap, and keep an eye on the Usage dashboard.

</details>

## Try it yourself: the 20-minute setup

The body lesson covered the dials and the data path. This is where you turn the dials yourself and look at what is on your dashboard. Twenty minutes if you take your time.

**Side effects:** no new charges (you are configuring caps, not spending). The settings take effect on the next message you send.

**Steps:**

1. **Open Settings, Budget.** Click the gear icon at the bottom of the navigation rail on the far left of the Clawless Computer window. In the sticky sidebar on the left of the Settings page, click the Budget section.

2. **Pick a monthly cap that feels comfortable.** Start with something low if you are nervous (the $5 or $10 options are common first picks). You can raise the cap at any time, and raising it unblocks immediately if you have already hit it. If you really do want no cap, the $0 (unlimited) option does what it says; CostGuard tracks the numbers but does not block.

3. **Choose your warn threshold.** Default is 80%. The 50%, 90%, and off options are all reasonable depending on temperament. The 80% default leaves enough headroom to react without nagging you too often.

4. **Choose your hard-stop behavior.** Default is Block (the next send is rejected when the cap is hit). The alternative is Warn and allow (every send over the cap shows a warning but the message goes through). Block is the safe pick unless you have a specific reason to allow overage.

5. **Save.** CostGuard is now active. If you use channels or scheduled jobs, remember the cap counts what they spend but does not stop them mid-run.

6. **Open the Usage dashboard.** Click the Usage icon on the navigation rail. Look at the breakdown by model, by conversation (the Sessions tab), and by day for the past week. If a model shows "Price not known" instead of a dollar amount, its spending is not counted toward your cap. If there is one conversation that ate disproportionate budget, that is the most important number on the screen; investigate it.

7. **Find your data folder and the About section.** In Settings, open System, then Your data. The line "Your data folder" shows where everything lives, and "Show in Finder" opens it. Then scroll to the About section: it shows the license line for Clawless Computer (AGPL-3.0) and the OpenClaw engine (MIT), and its Documentation button opens the help chapters, including "What is Under the Hood". The questions worth answering for yourself: who can read my conversations, what leaves my computer, and what happens to my data if I delete the app (the folder stays).

8. **Optional: try the calendar-month vs rolling-30-day toggle.** In Settings, Budget, switch the period mode if you would rather think in calendar months than in a rolling window. Both are reasonable; pick whichever matches how you already mentally track recurring costs.

**Expected outcome:** you finish with a monthly cap that matches your tolerance for surprise, a clear picture of what it stops and what it only counts, a real sense of where your money went the past week, and a current understanding of where your data lives and who can see it.

If something went sideways: the three first-day surprises at the end of the lesson body cover the most common confusions, especially the channels-keep-running and the data-path ones.
