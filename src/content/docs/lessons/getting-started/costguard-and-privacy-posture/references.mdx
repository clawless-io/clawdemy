## Source material

The lesson is anchored on five chapters from the [Clawless Computer](https://clawless.ai) knowledge base (Clawless Computer documentation, RBJ Global LLC), the single source of truth for Clawless Computer behavior. The KB is reachable from inside the app: open Settings, About, and click Documentation to read the help chapters on GitHub. The product itself lives at [clawless.ai](https://clawless.ai).

## Going deeper inside Clawless Computer

One follow-on KB chapter that touches what CostGuard and the privacy posture do not cover directly.

- **Clawless Computer Knowledge Base, "Chat and Models" chapter.** The model picker and choosing a model. Picking smaller models for routine work is the one of the biggest levers on your BYOK spend, and that chapter is where to look when you want to make that decision deliberately.

## Going deeper outside Clawless Computer

Three outside sources for readers who want context on local-first design, AI provider data policies, and personal AI spending discipline.

- **["Local-first software"](https://www.inkandswitch.com/local-first/)** (Ink and Switch, 2019). The original essay defining the local-first design principles that the Clawless Computer privacy posture follows. Reads as a manifesto, but the seven principles in section 3 are a useful checklist for evaluating any tool that claims to be local-first. The "your data stays on your computer" framing in this lesson sits inside the broader local-first lineage this essay defines.

- **[Anthropic privacy policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/privacy)** and **[OpenAI privacy policy](https://openai.com/policies/privacy-policy)**. Two of the major providers Clawless Computer can route through. Read both at least once if any of your messages contain anything you would not want a model trained on. The Clawless Computer documentation notes that most providers default to not training on API customer data, but the wording matters and the policies evolve, so the read-once-then-revisit cadence is the right one.
