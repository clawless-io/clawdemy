Two anxieties tend to surface in the first week with [Clawless Computer](https://clawless.ai). The first is "what if I accidentally rack up a giant bill?" The second is "who is actually seeing this conversation?"

Both are fair. Both have specific answers. This lesson covers them.

About twelve minutes to read. No code, no setup beyond what you already have.

## The cost worry, and what CostGuard does about it

You read in lesson 3 that Clawless Computer is BYOK: you bring your own API key, and the AI provider bills you directly for every message. That model is honest and it has no markup. It also means there is nothing standing between you and a real charge on your card if something goes wrong.

Most of the time nothing goes wrong. You type, the agent answers, the cost is fractions of a cent. But "most of the time" is not "always." A long agent loop that decides to do twenty tool calls in a row, a runaway conversation that takes a wrong turn, an experimental prompt that produces a 10,000-word reply: any of these can be more expensive than you expected. Not catastrophic. But more than zero.

**CostGuard is the safety net.** You set a monthly spending cap, and CostGuard keeps track of what you spend against it. If you get close to your cap, you get a warning. If you hit it, the default setting blocks new messages until you raise the cap or older spending ages out of the period. It is a small piece of software whose entire job is preventing the kind of surprise that ruins a good day.

Like any net, it has edges. It stops what Clawless Computer itself starts. A few kinds of work it counts but cannot stop, and a few it cannot count at all. A section below walks through them, so you know exactly where the net is.

## Setting your budget

Open Settings from the gear icon at the bottom of the navigation rail on the far left. In the sticky sidebar on the left of the page, click the Budget section.

Three settings to pick.

**Monthly cap.** A dropdown with sensible options: $0 (unlimited, equivalent to no cap), $5, $10, $25, $50, $100, or a custom amount. Start somewhere low if you are nervous. You can raise the cap at any time, and raising it unblocks immediately if you have already hit a previous lower one.

**Warn threshold.** When to notify you that you are getting close. The options are 50%, 80% (default), 90%, or off. The 80% default works for most people: enough headroom to react, not so frequent that you tune it out.

**Hard-stop behavior.** What happens when you hit 100% of the cap. The default is "Block new messages": the next send is rejected with a friendly banner saying you have reached your budget, and you have to raise the cap (or wait for the period to reset) to keep going. The alternative is "Warn and allow": every send over the cap shows a warning, but the message still goes through. Pick this if you would rather be informed than stopped.

Save the three settings and CostGuard is active.

## What counts toward the cap

The cap counts what Clawless Computer can put a price on.

- **Pay-as-you-go API keys** (Anthropic, Google AI, Groq, OpenAI, and most other providers). The cost of every message counts toward the cap.
- **Local models** (running on your own computer, for example through Ollama or LM Studio). These are free. They show as "Local, free" and never count.

Two things are not counted. Your provider still charges you for them, so the cap cannot stop what they spend.

- **Models whose price is not known.** These are custom model setups you added without a price, and some new models. Their use shows as "Price not known" with the model's name, not as a dollar amount. With Block on, Clawless Computer asks before sending most messages to such a model, because what it spends will not count.
- **Image generation.** There are no per-picture prices yet, so pictures are not counted. Each picture is billed to that provider's account. The chat around it is counted.

## What the cap can stop, and what it cannot

The cap stops everything Clawless Computer itself starts. That covers what you type in the chat box. It covers "Run now" on a scheduled job and compacting a conversation. It covers saying yes when the agent asks "Allow this action?". It also covers the app's own background work, such as sorting new memories. Saying no to an action always goes through, even over the cap. Refusing an action is a safety control, so it is never blocked.

Some work runs inside the engine without going through Clawless Computer. Two features in the app work this way. Channels let an agent answer you inside a messaging app such as Telegram, Discord, or Slack. Scheduled jobs let an agent run a task on a timetable you set. Both have their own icons on the navigation rail (Channels, and Cron for scheduled jobs). So replies in a connected channel run this way. So do scheduled jobs running on their schedule. So do turns an agent starts by itself, such as a sub-agent working on part of a task.

What these spend is counted in your total, so you reach the cap sooner. But the cap cannot stop them while they run. If you use channels or scheduled jobs, keep an eye on the Usage dashboard, and pause them when you get close to your cap.

One more exception, and it only matters if you switch it on. The engine has its own control page that opens in a web browser. It is off unless you turn it on under Settings, Security. If you leave it off, nothing here changes for you. If you turn it on, the spending limit does not apply to anything you do on that page. The reason, in plain words: that page talks straight to the engine, so the guards Clawless Computer puts in front of the engine stop applying there. The settings confirmations and the link and picture locks are skipped on that page too.

## What happens at the warn line and the hard line

Two visual cues track your spend through the month.

When you cross the warn threshold (80% by default), a notification appears once. The dock row indicator turns yellow as a quiet visual signal that you are getting close. Nothing else changes.

When you reach 100% of the cap, behavior depends on the hard-stop setting you picked.

With Block on (the default), the next message you send is rejected. A friendly banner appears in the chat:

```text
Monthly budget reached. Bump your cap in Settings to keep going.
```

The dock row indicator goes red. Sends stay blocked until you raise the cap (which unblocks immediately) or the next reset comes (which unblocks at the new period).

With Warn-and-allow on, every send over the cap shows a warning, but messages still go through. Use this mode if you have a specific reason to allow a temporary overage and you would rather get the warnings than the wall.

## How the period resets

The default is a rolling 30-day window. Every time you send a message, Clawless Computer looks back 30 days and sums the cost; if the total is under your cap, the message goes through. This means there is no "reset day" you have to wait for; cost from 31 days ago aged out as of today.

If you would rather the budget reset on the first of every month (the calendar-month model that matches most monthly subscriptions), you can change to calendar-month resets in Settings, Budget. Both modes are reasonable; pick the one that matches how you mentally track recurring costs.

## A few habits that keep costs down

A short list of small things that compound.

**Pick the right model for the task.** Heavyweight models cost five to ten times what smaller ones do per token. A quick factual question to a top-tier model is the same answer at a fraction of the cost on a smaller one. Lesson 2 walked the model picker; lesson 3 of Track 22 ("Choosing your model and the effort dial") goes deeper on the per-task economics if you want them.

**Start a fresh conversation when the topic changes.** Long conversations carry the entire prior thread as context with every new message. The cost grows roughly linearly with conversation length. A long tangential thread costs more than two shorter focused ones.

**Watch the Usage dashboard.** The Usage panel on the navigation rail breaks down spend by provider, by model, by session (the panel's word for a conversation), and by day. After your first week with Clawless Computer, opening it once tells you a lot. Sometimes the answer to "where did my budget go?" is "one specific session ate everything," and that tells you exactly what to change.

## Now the privacy half: where your data actually goes

The cost question is one half of the worry. The other half is "who sees this conversation?" The honest answer is short. It is also worth understanding clearly, because it is the most important thing to know about Clawless Computer after you know how to send a message.

When you send a message in Clawless Computer, the path is:

1. Your message goes from the chat input to the Clawless Computer app on your computer.
2. The app passes it to an open-source engine called OpenClaw, which is bundled with Clawless Computer.
3. OpenClaw sends the message to the AI provider you have selected, over the internet.
4. The provider's model thinks, generates a reply, and streams it back.
5. OpenClaw passes the reply to Clawless Computer.
6. Clawless Computer renders the reply in the chat.

Nothing in that path goes through a server of ours. The path is: your computer, the AI provider, your computer. There is no Clawless Computer cloud holding your conversations.

## Where your stuff actually lives

Two places matter, and it is worth being clear about both.

**Your computer.** This is where most of your data lives, all in one data folder. Your API keys. Your conversations. Your memories (from lesson 6). Your agent definitions. Your settings. To see where the folder is, open Settings, then System, then Your data. One thing to know about the keys: the OpenClaw engine stores them inside that folder, not encrypted, readable only by your user account. Clawless Computer never shows a saved key on screen. Tools the assistant runs on your computer run as your account too, so they could read the same files. Everything in that folder is as safe as the computer it sits on. For stronger protection, turn on your operating system's disk encryption (FileVault on a Mac, BitLocker on Windows). To back the folder up, quit Clawless Computer and copy the whole folder somewhere safe. That copy holds your API keys too, so keep it somewhere only you can reach.

**The AI provider's servers.** Every time you send a message, the message and any context (your memories that travel with it, the prior conversation, any attachments) go to the provider's servers. They process it on their side and stream back a reply. What the provider does with your messages after that depends on their terms; most providers default to not training on API customer data, but read their terms anyway, especially if your messages contain anything sensitive.

**A few other connections, none carrying your conversations.** The app contacts a few other services for narrow jobs. It fetches the community skill catalog when you open or search it. It downloads an add-on when you set up a channel that needs one. It checks for updates when you ask, or if you turn on automatic checks. None of these carry your conversations, memories, or API keys. If you connect a messaging channel, or your agent uses web search or the browser, those services are contacted on your behalf. Clawless Computer adds no telemetry, analytics, or crash reporting of its own.

## What you are trusting to whom

A useful framing. When you use Clawless Computer:

- You trust **us** to ship a desktop app that does what it claims and protects your local data to the standards your operating system supports.
- You trust **the AI provider** with the contents of your messages. They see what you type and what your memories carry along.
- You trust **OpenClaw** (the open-source engine) to be a faithful intermediary. Because it is open source, you can audit the code yourself; if you do not, you are trusting the maintainers and our review of their work.
- You trust **any tool, skill, or integration you install** with whatever permissions it asks for. That is the same trust model you would apply to any browser extension or app plugin.

This is roughly the same trust model as any software that uses third-party AI APIs. The short version: pick providers you actually trust with the contents of your messages, and be cautious about installing tools and skills from sources you do not recognize.

## What happens if Clawless Computer goes away

This is worth saying out loud because the question comes up often.

Your conversations, memories, and settings are on your computer. They are not in any Clawless Computer cloud. If the company went away tomorrow, your data would still be there, in the same place it has always been, readable by you. The app would keep working for as long as your local install runs and the AI providers you use stay online. There is no license server and no account, so there is nothing to lock you out. The data folder also stays put when you reinstall or update the app, and deleting the app does not delete it.

The strongest version of this guarantee is local models. Clawless Computer can run models entirely on your own computer with no cloud provider in the loop. The app launches and runs offline, and with a local model you can chat with no internet at all. That doubles as the answer to "what if the internet is down" and the answer to "what if I need a setup that does not depend on anyone else's servers."

## Common first-day surprises

Five things people often notice in the first week.

1. **Channel replies and scheduled jobs keep running after you hit the cap.** This is how the cap works, not a bug. What they spend still counts toward your total, but the cap cannot stop them mid-run. Pause them yourself when you get close.

2. **The dock row indicator changes color at the warn line, not at the hard line.** Yellow at 80% (default), red at 100%. If you only ever look at the red signal, you will be reacting at the worst possible moment.

3. **Raising the cap unblocks immediately.** You do not have to "save up" to the new cap. The next send goes through the moment Settings is saved.

4. **The numbers on the Usage dashboard come from Clawless Computer's own per-message tracking, not from the provider's billing system.** They should match the provider's invoice closely, but if there is ever a discrepancy, the provider's bill is the legal truth; ours is a tracking signal that helps you make decisions in real time.

5. **Your conversations are on your computer, not in a cloud you can sign into from another device.** There is no sync between computers. To move to a new Mac, you back up the data folder on the old one and restore it on the new one. The local-first model trades cross-device sync for the no-cloud guarantee.

## What you should remember

- **CostGuard is your spending safety net.** Set a monthly cap, a warn threshold (default 80%), and a hard-stop behavior (default Block). Pay-as-you-go provider calls count toward the cap; local models are free and do not count. Models with no known price and image generation are not counted.
- **The cap stops what Clawless Computer itself starts.** Channel replies, scheduled jobs, and turns an agent starts on its own count toward your total, but the cap cannot stop them while they run. The engine's own control page is off unless you turn it on; if you do, the spending limit does not apply to what you do on that page.
- **The dock row indicator tells you where you are.** Yellow at the warn threshold, red at 100%. The banner at 100% reads "Monthly budget reached. Bump your cap in Settings to keep going."
- **The data path is: your computer, the AI provider, your computer.** There is no Clawless Computer cloud holding your conversations. Two places matter: your computer (where most things live, in one data folder) and the AI provider's servers (where your message goes to be answered).
- **If Clawless Computer went away tomorrow, your data stays.** Conversations, memories, and settings are on your computer. There is no license server and no account to lock you out. Local models let you keep working offline if you ever need to.

In the practice that goes with this lesson, you set a monthly cap that feels comfortable, open the Usage dashboard to see what your week looked like, and find your data folder and the app's About section. About twenty minutes start to finish.
