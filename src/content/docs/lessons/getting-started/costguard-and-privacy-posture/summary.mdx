## The short version

Two anxieties on the first week with [Clawless Computer](https://clawless.ai): "what if I rack up a giant bill?" and "who is actually seeing this conversation?" Both have specific answers. **CostGuard** is the spending safety net: you set a monthly cap, a warn threshold, and a hard-stop behavior, and CostGuard tracks your BYOK spending against it. Pay-as-you-go API calls count toward the cap; local models are free and do not. With Block on (the default), the cap can stop what Clawless Computer itself starts; channel replies and scheduled jobs count toward your total but cannot be stopped while they run. The data path is short: your computer, the AI provider, your computer. There is no Clawless Computer server holding your conversations, and there is no license server and no account. If the company behind Clawless Computer ever shut down, your conversations, memories, and settings stay on your computer because that is where they have always been. Local models keep the app useful even with no internet.

## Core ideas

- **The cost worry is real but bounded.** A long agent loop, a runaway conversation, or an experimental prompt that produces a 10,000-word reply can cost more than you expected. Not catastrophic; more than zero. CostGuard exists for exactly this.
- **CostGuard is a software safety net** that tracks your spending against a monthly cap. With the default setting it blocks new messages once you reach the cap, until you raise it or older spending ages out of the period.
- **Three settings in Settings, Budget.** Monthly cap (dropdown: $0 / $5 / $10 / $25 / $50 / $100 / custom). Warn threshold (50% / 80% default / 90% / off). Hard-stop behavior (Block new messages by default; alternative Warn-and-allow passes the message through with a warning).
- **What counts toward the cap.** The cap counts what Clawless Computer can put a price on. Pay-as-you-go API keys (Anthropic, Google AI, Groq, OpenAI, most others): every message's cost counts. Local models running on your computer (for example through Ollama or LM Studio): shown as "Local, free", never counted.
- **What is not counted.** Models whose price is not known (custom setups without a price, some new models) show as "Price not known" and do not count; with Block on, Clawless Computer asks before sending most messages to them. Image generation is not counted either. Your provider still bills you for both.
- **What the cap can stop, and what it cannot.** With Block on (the default), the cap can stop everything Clawless Computer itself starts: what you type in the chat box, "Run now" on a scheduled job, compacting a conversation when you ask for it, allowing an action the agent asks about, and the app's own background work. It is checked when work starts, so a run already under way can finish and is caught at your next message. Saying no to an action always goes through. Replies in a connected channel (Telegram, Discord, Slack and others), scheduled jobs running on their schedule, and turns an agent starts by itself run inside the engine: what they spend counts, so you reach the cap sooner, but the cap cannot stop them while they run. The cap also cannot stop some of the engine's own background work, like keeping a long conversation compact or searching memory. Watch the Usage dashboard and pause channels and scheduled jobs when you get close.
- **The engine control page is an opt-in exception.** The engine has its own browser page, off unless you turn it on under Settings, Security. If you turn it on, the spending limit does not apply to what you do on that page, because that page talks straight to the engine and skips the checks Clawless Computer puts in front of it (the spending limit, the settings confirmations, and the link and picture locks).
- **Banners on the home screen track your spend.** An **amber banner at the warn threshold** (80% default), with a notification shown once. A **red banner at 100%** of the cap; with Block on (default), the next send is rejected with a friendly banner in the chat. With Warn-and-allow on, every send over the cap shows a warning but messages still go through.
- **Unblocking is fast.** Raising the cap in Settings, Budget unblocks immediately on save. There is no waiting period and no "save up to the new cap" rule.
- **The period defaults to a rolling 30-day window.** Cost from 31 days ago aged out today. The alternative is calendar-month resets (matches most monthly subscription billing). Both reasonable; pick the one that matches how you mentally track recurring costs.
- **Habits that compound to lower costs.** Pick the right model for the task (heavyweight models cost five to thirty times what smaller ones do per token). Start a fresh conversation when the topic changes (long threads carry the full history with every new message). Watch the Usage dashboard (it breaks spend down by model, by conversation (the Sessions tab), and by day; sometimes one specific conversation ate everything, and that tells you what to change).
- **The data path is short.** Your message goes from chat input to the Clawless Computer app on your computer, to the bundled open-source OpenClaw engine (still on your computer), to the AI provider over the internet, and the reply streams back the same way. **No Clawless Computer server in the data path.**
- **Two places matter for where your stuff lives.** Your computer (the bulk of it, in one data folder you can find under Settings, then System, then Your data: API keys, conversations, memories, agent definitions, and settings. The OpenClaw engine stores your API keys in that folder, not encrypted, readable only by your user account, and Clawless Computer never shows a saved key. Tools the assistant runs on your computer run as your account too. Turn on FileVault on a Mac or BitLocker on Windows for stronger at-rest protection). The AI provider's servers (your messages and the memories that travel with them; what they retain after that is governed by their terms).
- **Other connections carry no conversations.** The skill catalog when you open or search it, add-on downloads when you set up a channel, and update checks when you ask for them carry none of your conversations, memories, or API keys. Channels and tools you set up (web search, the browser) contact those services on your behalf. Clawless Computer adds no telemetry, analytics, or crash reporting.
- **The four-party trust model.** You trust us to ship a desktop app that does what it claims and protects local data to the standards your operating system supports. You trust the AI provider with the contents of your messages and the memories that travel with them. You trust OpenClaw (the open-source engine) to be a faithful intermediary; the code is open for you to audit, and if you do not, you are trusting the maintainers and our review of their work. You trust any tool, skill, or integration you install with whatever permissions it asks for, the same model as browser extensions.
- **Two projects, two licences.** Clawless Computer, the desktop app, is free software under the GNU AGPL v3; Settings, About links to its source code on GitHub. OpenClaw, the engine bundled inside it, is a separate open-source project under the MIT license. They are not the same project. The data path crosses both, and both can be read in full.
- **If Clawless Computer went away tomorrow, your data stays.** Conversations, memories, settings, and agent definitions are on your computer disk. They do not vanish when the company does. The app keeps working as long as the local install runs and the AI providers stay online. There is no license server and no account, so there is nothing to lock you out. The data folder stays when you reinstall or update, and deleting the app does not delete it.
- **Local models are the strongest version of the no-cloud guarantee.** The app launches and runs offline, and with a local model you can chat with no cloud provider in the loop. Doubles as the answer for "what if I am on a plane" and "what if I need a setup that depends on no one else's servers."

## What changes for you

Before this lesson, "what is the worst that can happen if I screw up" probably had no specific answer in your head. Now it does: for everything Clawless Computer itself starts, the default setting stops new paid work at the cap you picked, with a friendly banner when you reach it, and you know which kinds of work (channel replies, scheduled jobs, some of the engine's own background work, image generation, unpriced models) to watch yourself. And "who actually sees this" has a specific answer too: the AI provider you picked, plus any channel or tool you connect yourself, because there is no Clawless Computer server holding conversations. The most useful new habit is **setting the cap to something low enough that it occasionally trips**. A cap that never trips is a number on a screen; a cap that occasionally trips is teaching you what your real usage shape looks like. After the first time, you raise it intentionally rather than fearfully, and you have learned more about your own AI workflow than any dashboard could tell you.
